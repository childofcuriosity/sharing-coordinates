\begin{abstract}
When layers share learned bases $B$ through a simplex router $A$, the effective
weights $\Theta=AB$ need not determine the factors or their training
response. We ask whether optimizer response identifies these factors and
how much of that information is needed to choose a hard-sharing partition. For positive full-rank softmax factors on the same product
fiber, complete response under fixed known positive Euclidean block rates
and temperature is identical if and only if the factors differ by a common
permutation. On uniformly nondegenerate domains we give an explicit inverse
bound and, when $D-K\ge L$, a whole-feasible-set diameter bound from a noisy
product and $K$ designed matrix-valued response queries, under a stated
product-noise threshold. Choosing a partition requires less information than recovering the factors:
balanced router-vector clustering minimizes full Hilbert--Schmidt response
error, and off-diagonal router Gram entries suffice to select a minimizer. It can conflict with weight clustering, even across
factorizations with the same effective product. We construct a fixed
squared loss whose gradient flow separates coefficient assignment from weight clustering, and
give conditional agreement and trajectory-error bounds. In controlled experiments on nine pretrained language models, factor
recovery error was below $0.034$ in all 1,080 cases meeting the specified
rank conditions. A separate three-model study found decision crossings
during training. Local SGD from a selected real-model state and a
controlled regression experiment reproduced crossings across numerical
precisions and step sizes. The results establish identification under the
specified optimizer and show that training can separate sharing decisions.
They leave open how often crossings occur and whether full factor recovery
improves downstream performance; they do not identify historical sharing
graphs or extend the identification theorem to AdamW.
\end{abstract}
